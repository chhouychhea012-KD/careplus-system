1. Executive vision
The hospital does not need a collection of unrelated CRUD pages. It needs one connected, safe, practical
workflow in which staff can manage a patient from first contact through consultation, departmental care,
payment, discharge and follow-up.
Client success question: Can hospital staff complete a real patient journey quickly, accurately, and without
confusion, while maintaining correct patient and encounter context?
2. Assumed hospital profile
Dimension
Working assumption
Institution
Private general hospital, 24/7 urgent services and scheduled outpatient departments
Departments
General medicine, pediatrics, OB/GYN, surgery, emergency, laboratory, radiology, pharmacy,
inpatient wards, billing and administration
Channels
Desktop web for hospital staff; Telegram Mini App for doctors and patients
Localization
Khmer and English, locally appropriate dates/times, print layouts
Architecture approach
Use existing Spring Boot + Thymeleaf + Tailwind codebase; in-memory prototype now, Oracle later
3. Target patient journey
1. Appointment booking or walk-in
2. Registration and identity verification
3. Arrival/check-in and encounter initiation
4. Triage and prioritized department queue
5. Doctor consultation and longitudinal medical chart
6. Optional laboratory/imaging orders and result review
7. Prescription/pharmacy and billing
8. Discharge, admission, referral or follow-up
Clinical exceptions are mandatory: emergency routing, doctor or department transfer, return-to-doctor after
results, missed queue calls, optional steps, pending results, repeat visits, and admission. Do not force all
patients through every stage.

6. High-priority clinical interface: Doctor Workspace
A doctor must manage a single active encounter from one integrated workspace, opened from an appointment,
doctor queue, or patient chart. The patient header and visit context should remain visible.
6.1 Always-visible information
 • Patient name and unique ID, age/sex, encounter and appointment numbers, department and assigned doctor.
 • Allergies or critical alerts, queue number, current visit status and recent vitals.
 • Previous relevant history without losing the active note or order draft.

6.2 Main workspace tabs
Overview; Medical History; Triage and Vitals; Consultation; Clinical Notes; Diagnosis; Laboratory Orders;
Imaging; Prescriptions; Medical Certificates; Documents; Encounter Timeline; Follow-Up and Discharge.
6.3 Key doctor actions
 • Start consultation; save draft; add note or diagnosis; order lab/imaging; review results; prescribe; create medical
 certificate; arrange follow-up; complete visit.
• Always prefill the correct patient/encounter; do not ask clinicians to search again for the same patient.
 • Protect signed notes and reviewed clinical records from silent overwrites; display blockers before encounter
 completion.
7. Appointment details as the central visit entry point
 • Display patient header, doctor, department, schedule, reason, appointment status and linked encounter.
 • Show step progression: registration, check-in, triage, consultation, optional orders/results, completion. Derive
 statuses from data rather than static UI.
• Provide direct access to medical history, vitals, prior notes, results, prescriptions, certificates and follow-up.
 • Offer context-appropriate quick actions; prevent invalid actions in incompatible states or for unauthorized roles.
 
8. Queue management UX
 • Use department and role-specific queues: registration, triage, consultation, lab, imaging, pharmacy and
 payment.
• Clearly show waiting, called, in service, on hold, transferred, completed, missed and cancelled states.
 • Each row/card: queue number, patient identifier, arrival/wait time, priority, doctor, department, status and next
 action.
• Support call patient, call next, hold, resume, transfer, mark missed, start consultation and complete current
 step.
• Keep encounter open appropriately during transfers; a queue transfer is not necessarily a clinical discharge.

11. Telegram Mini Apps and QR requirements
Provide two mobile experiences, both sharing authorized patient and encounter data with the web system. A
QR code opens the Mini App; a separate, scoped QR flow checks a patient in.
11.1 Doctor Mini App
 • Today's appointments, assigned queue, current patient, pending notes, results and follow-up tasks.
 • Compact authorized patient summary, allergies, vitals, recent history and consultation actions.
 • Authentication and staff-role checks before exposing medical information.
 
11.2 Patient Mini App
 • Book, view, reschedule or cancel appointments according to policy.
 • View queue ticket and real status; patient arrival check-in using verified QR or code.
 • Access only appropriately released medical documents and personal visit history.
 
11.3 Two QR journeys
QR type
Purpose and protection
Mini App launch QR
Encode a configured Telegram Mini App/deep link; scanning opens Telegram, not privileged medical
access
Arrival/check-in QR
Verify patient/appointment, use scoped expiring token, prevent duplicate check-in and replay; issue
queue ticket only after verification
Telegram integration requires a configured bot, HTTPS hosting, server-side validation of Telegram initialization
data, secure account linking, role authorization and safe notification content. The demo may use synthetic
identities but must clearly distinguish demo from production.

12. Core non-functional requirements
 • Patient safety: positive identification, alert visibility, order status clarity, clinician review and correct record
 selection.
• Security: authenticated users, server-side permissions, encryption, least privilege and comprehensive audit.
 • Reliability: backups, restore testing, monitoring, recovery and documented downtime processes.
 • Data integrity: correct encounter relations; no orphaned queue tickets or silent loss of signed records.
 • Accessibility: readable typography, keyboard support, sufficient contrast, responsive layouts and usability
 testing.
• Interoperability readiness: align core concepts with Patient, Encounter, ServiceRequest, Observation,
 DiagnosticReport and MedicationRequest models; do not claim FHIR compliance without implementation.
• Localization: Khmer/English, local date/time handling and print-ready documents.

15. Proposed client sign-off checklist
 • Hospital leadership confirms hospital type, department structure, operating hours and actual patient pathways.
 • Clinical leads approve documentation, order, result release, prescribing and certificate policies.
 • Reception, nursing, doctor, lab, imaging, pharmacy and cashier roles pass usability walkthroughs.
 • Test data proves correct patient/encounter references across departments.
 • Privacy, access, clinical risk and operational recovery requirements receive appropriate review.
 • The visible production UI and test results are demonstrated in the running application, not only in screenshots or
 documentation.
Client acceptance statement: The hospital needs a system that helps staff deliver care with fewer unnecessary
clicks, clear actions and reliable shared records. Approval depends on successfully demonstrating a complete
patient visit in the real application.

Addendum 1
ADDENDUM A | WEB-BUILT MINI APPS AND TELEGRAM HOSPITAL BOT
Web Mini Apps, QR Phone Access and Hospital Bot
Document revision: Version 1.1 | 11 November 2026. This addendum supplements the existing eight-page E-Hospital client
requirements. It clarifies the technology and adds implementable acceptance criteria.
A1. Confirmed delivery model
The doctor and patient Mini Apps must be built as web applications using the existing Spring Boot, Thymeleaf, Tailwind CSS and
JavaScript stack. They are not native Android/iOS applications. The same hosted application must render comfortably on phones
through responsive, touch-friendly layouts, and be launchable inside Telegram using QR codes and bot links.
• Desktop web remains the main hospital administration and clinical management environment.
• Mini App screens are purpose-designed web views optimized for phone usage; they reuse the hospital backend and records rather than
maintaining a second data store.
• Provide a browser preview for local development; real Telegram access requires the configured public HTTPS deployment and Telegram bot.
• Changes made by authorized users in web or Mini App must be reflected in the same appointment, encounter, queue and patient records.

A3. Hospital bot - required capabilities
Create a hospital Telegram bot integration with configurable bot identity, bot token stored server-side, webhook endpoint,
command handlers, localization, and safe messaging. BotFather provisioning and Telegram credentials remain externally
configured.
Bot area

/start, /help, /language, /contact; welcome message, hospital location/hours, quick web-app launch controls.
Patient services
Launch appointment booking, upcoming bookings, arrival/check-in and queue status; send optional generic
reminders without exposing sensitive clinical details.

After secure staff account linking, launch assigned queue, today's schedule, tasks and results-review worklist; never
grant privileges from a QR alone.
Administrative operations
Configure bot username, link, webhook health, allowed notification templates, enable/disable options, logs, error
handling and QR poster printing.
A4. Authentication and privacy
• Verify Telegram Mini App initData on the Spring Boot server and check freshness; never trust initDataUnsafe or a frontend role switch.
• Link Telegram identities to hospital patient/staff records through an independently verified hospital login or approved account-linking
procedure.
• Use role- and patient-scoped server authorization, audit access and changes, and secure sessions.
• Never place medical records, diagnoses or sensitive documents into public QR tokens or routine Telegram chat notifications.
• Demo mode must be explicitly labeled and must not permit real clinical access.

Addendum 2
ADDENDUM B | IMPLEMENTATION, UI AND ACCEPTANCE
Mini App and Bot Delivery Requirements
B1. Patient web Mini App
• Home: next appointment, department, queue status and quick actions.
• Booking: search specialty/doctor, choose real available slot from shared service, confirm/reschedule/cancel when permitted.
• Arrival: phone-friendly scanner where supported, on-site QR validation, manual fallback and duplicate check-in prevention.
• Queue: ticket number, department, status and instructions; avoid ungrounded exact waiting-time estimates.
• Records: release-gated results, prescriptions, certificates and visit history, accessible only after verified authorization.
• Accessibility: English/Khmer, local Khmer font, dark/light themes, safe areas, 360-430 px phone widths, readable controls.
B2. Doctor web Mini App
• My day: assigned appointments, active consultations, waiting patients, pending clinical tasks.
• My queue: call patient, hold, transfer with clinician selection, start consultation, complete queue step.
• Patient context: verified assigned encounter, allergies, vitals, recent history, lab/imaging results and outstanding orders.
• Documentation: save or review authorized clinical note drafts, view certificate drafts, track signed items; apply clinical sign-off rules.
• Mobile usability: sticky patient identity, few primary actions, bottom navigation, robust navigation/back behavior and no horizontal overflow.
B3. Bot setup and QR management screen
• Admin path: Settings > Telegram Integration; connection status, webhook test, bot identity, Mini App URL and launch-link preview.
• Generate printable patient and doctor Mini App access QR posters from real configured Telegram deep links, with explicit role labels.
• Provide a public HTTPS host requirement and BotFather setup instructions; local localhost links must only be used for browser preview.
• Implement safe notification templates and consent/preferences, webhook signature/secret handling where configured, delivery history and
rate limits.
• No hard-coded bot tokens; secrets live in server environment/secret manager and never in templates or browser bundles.
B4. Acceptance tests for client sign-off
 Test
Expected result
Patient QR from phone
Scan printed QR -> Telegram Mini App opens (with configured bot/HTTPS) -> verified patient signs in -> books visit.
Hospital check-in
Patient checks in exactly once for an eligible appointment; correct visit and queue update appears on desktop.
Doctor QR from phone
Scanning QR opens Mini App; unauthorized user cannot see doctor workspace; verified assigned doctor sees own
queue.
Cross-channel consistency
Doctor queue action updates receptionist desktop and patient queue UI using the same record identifiers.
Accessibility and themes
Doctor/patient workflows pass Khmer/English, light/dark and narrow-screen tests without clipping.
Bot security
Invalid/stale Telegram initData rejected; no clinical data sent in chat notifications; secrets absent from frontend.
B5. Implementation and readiness milestones
Milestone 1 - Prototype: Responsive Mini App web screens, shared in-memory hospital workflows, browser preview, mock QR UI,
functional demo actions. Milestone 2 - Telegram sandbox: BotFather bot, public HTTPS, verified initData, secure account linking,
QR launch, bot commands and controlled notifications. Milestone 3 - Production readiness: Real authentication, database,
audit/security/privacy review, patient consent, monitoring, backups, testing on representative phones, and clinical approval.
Scope note: The bot and live Mini App integration are requirements to implement, not claims that they are already deployed. Real phone-based
Telegram tests require configured bot credentials and HTTPS hosting.
